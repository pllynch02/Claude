## 1. Community Loan Fund of New Jersey, Inc. and Subsidiaries (d/b/a New Jersey Community Capital)

1. **Entity name:** Community Loan Fund of New Jersey, Inc. and Subsidiaries (NJCC)
2. **Entity type:** U.S. Treasury-certified CDFI community loan fund (statewide NJ; also NMTC allocatee via sub-CDEs)
3. **Fiscal year / audit year:** FY ended September 30, 2024 (comparative 2023)
4. **Audit firm:** KPMG LLP (Short Hills, NJ; opinion dated January 28, 2025)
5. **Source:** `documents/222872262_2024.pdf` — https://www.newjerseycommunitycapital.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/CLFNJ-9-30-24-Audited-Financial-Statements.pdf (corroborated by FY2024 and FY2023 single audits, `documents/222872262_2024_singleaudit.pdf`, `documents/222872262_2023_singleaudit.pdf`)
6. **Exact quoted disclosure language:**
   - Note 2(m) *Funds Held in Trust, Escrows, and Other* (printed p. 20, PDF p. 22):
     > "Funds held in trust also include third-party resources entrusted to the Organization's oversight, generally for its Third Party Managed Assets programs. The Organization does not record the loans receivable associated with these programs in its consolidated financial statements as its responsibility is limited to servicing and/or administering the loans. The cash related to these programs that will be returned within one (1) year is included in cash and cash equivalents and amounts to approximately $1,009,000 and $841,000 at September 30, 2024 and 2023, respectively."
   - Note 1 *Operating Divisions* (printed pp. 12–13, PDF pp. 14–15):
     > "Third Party Managed Assets include loan pools administered by CLFNJ."
     > "The program is managed on behalf of the Housing and Community Development Network of New Jersey, a trade association serving the community development corporation sector. CLFNJ services and administers the program on behalf of the Housing and Community Development Network of New Jersey."
     > "The UEZ entered into a contractual relationship whereby CLFNJ services and administers the program on behalf of the UEZ."
     > "In 2005, Bank of America capitalized a pre-development loan fund for housing and real estate initiatives. … CLFNJ services and administers the program on behalf of Bank of America."
7. **F/S line item / note caption:** Liability caption "Funds held in trust, escrows, and other (note 10)" — $10,209,071 current and $4,826,362 long-term at 9/30/2024. Note 10 itemizes program balances (SEED funds $315,119; BofA funds $960,759; TICIC funds $2,560,097; GFI funds $426,538; escrows $5,146,417; conditional program advances).
8. **Presentation model:** Full off-balance-sheet treatment of administered loan pools; only cash temporarily held is recognized, as a "funds held in trust" liability. (Separately, NMTC sub-CDE loans are also off the consolidated statements: SPE limited partners "have substantive participating rights, and accordingly, the SPEs are accounted for using the equity method," Note 1, PDF p. 13.)
9. **Disclosure depth:** Detailed — dedicated accounting-policy paragraph, program-by-program description, and a liability rollout table.
10. **Tier:** A (CLFNJ is servicer/administrator; funders — a bank, a trade association, a municipal UEZ, philanthropic disaster funds — own the pools). Not shown to be lender of record.
11. **Why it supports the target fact pattern:** A large, KPMG-audited NFP CDFI administers loan pools capitalized by third parties (bank, government-adjacent, philanthropic), earns servicing/administration fees, and **explicitly excludes the loans receivable** from its statements while recognizing undisbursed/collected cash as an agency liability — precisely the presentation the target position asserts. The policy wording ("responsibility is limited to servicing and/or administering the loans") is directly reusable.
12. **Limitations / differences:** The programs are modest relative to NJCC's own $140M on-balance-sheet portfolio; disclosure does not discuss credit risk allocation explicitly (absence of loss-sharing is implied by "responsibility is limited to servicing"); CLFNJ is not stated to be lender of record on the administered pools.
13. **Confidence grade:** **A**

## 2. Community Vision Capital & Consulting (San Francisco, CA)

1. **Entity name:** Community Vision Capital & Consulting (f/k/a Northern California Community Loan Fund)
2. **Entity type:** CDFI loan fund and certified Community Development Entity (NMTC allocatee); manages third-party grant/loan pools
3. **Fiscal year / audit year:** FY ended September 30, 2025 (comparative 2024)
4. **Audit firm:** Hood & Strong LLP (San Francisco, CA)
5. **Source:** `documents/943032394_2025_singleaudit.pdf` — FAC single audit 2025-09-GSAFAC-0000398055 via https://app.fac.gov/dissemination/report/pdf/2025-09-GSAFAC-0000398055 (also `documents/943032394_2021.pdf` from communityvisionca.org)
6. **Exact quoted disclosure language:**
   - Note 3 *Funds Held in Trust* (printed pp. 15–16, PDF pp. 17–18):
     > "The Organization receives and distributes assets under certain mission-aligned intermediary arrangements. The Organization holds such funds as funds held in trust. Distributions of such funds are managed by the Organization according to the guidelines of the specific programs."
     > "The Organization manages targeted grant pools for varying purposes. The Organization underwrites and coordinates grants for these pools in accordance with the conditions imposed by the original source of the funds. The Organization also disburses, monitors, tracks, and reports on these grants."
   - Note 1 program description (printed p. 8, PDF p. 10):
     > "The department also evaluates, designs, and implements innovative new programs and strategies and manages a number of mission-aligned grant and loan programs on behalf of private foundations, local government agencies, and other third parties."
   - Note 14 *New Markets Tax Credit Fees* (printed pp. 29–30): allocations "totaling $218,000,000 (cumulatively)"; 26 sub-CDE LLCs formed — the consolidated statements carry **no NMTC loan assets**, only "New markets tax credit fees" revenue of $288,633 (2025).
7. **F/S line item / note caption:** "Funds held in trust (Note 3)" appears as **both** an asset ($7,454,920) and an identical liability ($7,454,920) at 9/30/2025 ($2,609,707 in 2024) — a gross agency asset with exactly matching agency liability. Note 3 rollforward shows "Grant and administrative fees" retained by the Organization ($707,200 in 2024).
8. **Presentation model:** Gross agency asset with matching agency liability for administered intermediary pools; full exclusion (deconsolidation) of NMTC sub-CDE loan portfolios, fee income only.
9. **Disclosure depth:** Moderate — dedicated note with rollforward, but no explicit statement about loans receivable exclusion; the loan-program component of the intermediary arrangements is not separately quantified.
10. **Tier:** A (intermediary/administrator for foundations and local government agencies)
11. **Why it supports the target fact pattern:** Demonstrates the "funds held in trust" agency model at a major CDFI: third-party program money appears only as cash-in/cash-out with a matching liability, the entity keeps administrative fees, and $218M of NMTC allocation deployed through managed sub-CDEs never grosses up its statements.
12. **Limitations / differences:** Note 3 language emphasizes grant pools more than loan pools (the loan-program administration is described in the program narrative, not the accounting note); NMTC exclusion rests on the sub-CDEs being separate for-profit LLCs owned 99.99% by investors rather than on agency accounting.
13. **Confidence grade:** **B** (strong for the gross-agency and fee-only models; less explicit than NJCC on loans-receivable exclusion)

## 3. Appalachian Community Capital Corporation (incl. Green Bank for Rural America)

1. **Entity name:** Appalachian Community Capital Corporation and Affiliates (consolidating ACC Development Foundation and Green Bank for Rural America)
2. **Entity type:** Wholesale CDFI intermediary (lends to member CDFIs); EPA Greenhouse Gas Reduction Fund (Clean Communities Investment Accelerator) award recipient via Green Bank for Rural America
3. **Fiscal year / audit year:** FY ended December 31, 2024 (comparative 2023)
4. **Audit firm:** Brown, Edwards & Company, L.L.P.
5. **Source:** `documents/462815132_2024.pdf` — https://appalachiancommunitycapitalcdfi.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/12.24-Report-ACCC-Final.pdf (also FY2023 and FY2025 single audits in `documents/`)
6. **Exact quoted disclosure language:**
   - Note 1 (printed p. 8, PDF p. 10):
     > "In 2024, the Company received a grant award for $500,000,000 from the Environmental Protection Agency (EPA), as part of the Inflation Reduction Act. The Company formed Green Bank for Rural America to accelerate economic restructuring and diversification in communities impacted by declines in the fossil fuel industry. As a result of this grant award, these funds of approximately $500.7 million are included in the restricted cash and cash equivalents line on the statement of condition with a corresponding amount as deferred revenue."
7. **F/S line item / note caption:** "Deferred revenue" $520,262,505 at 12/31/2024 (vs. $1,398,327 in 2023) against restricted cash; ACC's own on-balance-sheet lending is small by comparison ("Loans to CDFI's $31,690,293," Note 3).
8. **Presentation model:** Conditional grant / refundable-advance liability model for a federal climate-lending capital pool: the $500M is a liability (deferred revenue), not net assets or revenue, until conditions are met through deployment.
9. **Disclosure depth:** Moderate — one explicit paragraph tying the award to restricted cash and deferred revenue.
10. **Tier:** A-adjacent (the capital is federal, repayable-in-substance until conditions met; ACC/GBRA earns its economics through the program rather than owning the capital outright at receipt)
11. **Why it supports the target fact pattern:** Directly supports the "government grant-funded layer" leg: a $500M federal lending-program capitalization sits wholly on the liability side under ASC 958-605 conditional-contribution logic, demonstrating that receiving and administering government loan-program capital does not create net assets (or, in agency variants, any balance at all) until the entity's obligations are discharged.
12. **Limitations / differences:** This is conditional-contribution accounting, not agency accounting — the funds will eventually become ACC/GBRA revenue as conditions are met, unlike true funds-held-for-others; ACC grosses up its own member loans.
13. **Confidence grade:** **B** (category-specific: grant-funded layer / liability-until-deployed)

   - Note 2.I *Investments* (printed p. 12):
     > "During 2023, the Company formed four limited liability partnerships related to the CWCOT program. The Company is the general partner with a 0.0001% ownership interest. The Company does not receive any financial benefit from the limited partnerships, therefore, no activity has been recorded in the accompanying financial statements as of and for the years ended December 31, 2024 and 2023."
   - Note 2.H on CalHome (printed pp. 11–12): "the CalHome loans were made from grant funds that may allow forgiveness of all or a portion of accrued interest" and "Loan repayments revolve into a restricted reuse account which funds future loans."

10. **Tier:** A

12. **Limitations / differences:** The CalHome treatment cuts the other way (grant-funded revolving loans recognized as own assets when the entity controls the revolved funds) — cite carefully; administered portfolio sizes are not quantified in the statements.
13. **Confidence grade:** **B–/C+** (good narrative support; thin quantification)

## 6. Florida Development Finance Corporation (FDFC)

1. **Entity name:** Florida Development Finance Corporation
2. **Entity type:** State-created development finance authority; conduit bond issuer and PACE bond facilitator (files Form 990; reports under GASB as a component unit of the State of Florida)
3. **Fiscal year / audit year:** FY ended June 30, 2021 (latest audit posted with the Florida Auditor General)
4. **Audit firm:** (independent auditor's report in `documents/593270584_2021.pdf`, filed with the Florida Auditor General under §288.9610, Fla. Stat.)
5. **Source:** `documents/593270584_2021.pdf` — https://flauditor.gov/pages/nonprofit_forprofit%20rpts/2021%20florida%20development%20finance%20corporation.pdf
6. **Exact quoted disclosure language:**
   - Note 5 *Conduit Debt* (printed p. 12, PDF p. 16):
     > "In accordance with its mission and Chapter 288, Part X, Florida Statutes, FDFC has facilitated the issuance of debt obligations whereby FDFC is merely a conduit issuer of bonds issued on behalf of borrowers. These bonds do not constitute a general debt, liability or obligation of FDFC, the State of Florida, or any local government. Additionally, FDFC has assigned all rights to receive payments from the borrowers to the bond purchaser in all bond financing transactions. Assigned payments are not included in the accompanying basic financial statements."
7. **F/S line item / note caption:** No conduit assets or liabilities on the statement of net position (total assets ~$12M vs. $107.3M PACE conduit debt plus hundreds of millions of other conduit bonds disclosed in Note 5 rollforward tables); revenue is "Conduit debt application and issuance fees $1,843,314" and "PACE program fees $730,393."
8. **Presentation model:** Full exclusion of both the financed receivables and the matching debt; note-only disclosure of outstanding conduit balances; fee-only income statement.
9. **Disclosure depth:** Detailed (dedicated note with per-issue rollforward tables).
10. **Tier:** B-flavored — FDFC **is the issuer of record** of the bonds (its name is on the debt) yet records neither the debt nor the assigned repayment streams.
11. **Why it supports the target fact pattern:** The strongest issuer-of-record netting in the set: legal obligor status does not drive recognition where repayment comes solely from borrowers and rights to collections are assigned away — squarely the "repaid solely from borrower collections, no general recourse" logic, with fee-only revenue.
12. **Limitations / differences:** GASB reporter (conduit-debt treatment is codified in governmental GAAP — now GASB 91), so for a FASB NFP it is an analogy, not on-point FASB precedent; PACE repayment runs through tax assessments rather than a conventional loan waterfall.
13. **Confidence grade:** **C+** as FASB precedent (labeled analogy), **A** within its own framework for the full-exclusion category

## 8. Montgomery County Green Bank Corporation (MD)

   - Partner-lender credit enhancement (capped first-loss; partner loans off-book):

This category includes funds that are held by the Organization for the purpose of reimbursing the lender for future loans which may default. 8. **Presentation model:** Funds held for others (county capital) + liability-until-earned (federal climate subgrant) + full exclusion of partner-lender loan portfolios supported only by a capped loss-reserve liability.
9. **Disclosure depth:** Detailed.
10. **Tier:** A
11. **Why it supports the target fact pattern:** A green bank that (i) books government revolving capital as an explicit "funds held on behalf of" agency liability because unspent funds revert to the government, (ii) holds federal lending capital as deferred revenue until deployed, and (iii) supports third-party-funded loans it does not record, exposing only its capped first-loss — the exact credit-risk architecture (nil-or-capped first loss) of the target pattern.
12. **Limitations / differences:** The loans it originates itself are recognized; the partner-lender loans were never MCGB's (origination by banks), so the exclusion is less contested than the target pattern.
13. **Confidence grade:** **A–** (multi-model support, especially for government capital and capped first-loss)
