# Synthesis — Published Precedent for Net / Agency / Off-Balance-Sheet Presentation of Third-Party-Funded Loan Programs

All citations refer to `precedent_table.md` records (R1–R13) and documents in `documents/`. Quotes are verbatim from retrieved documents.

## 1. Strongest examples overall

1. **Community Loan Fund of New Jersey (R1, KPMG, FY2024)** — the anchor precedent. A dedicated accounting-policy paragraph states the exact position: *"The Organization does not record the loans receivable associated with these programs in its consolidated financial statements as its responsibility is limited to servicing and/or administering the loans."* Third-party pools (bank, trade-association, municipal, philanthropic capital) are off-balance-sheet; only cash temporarily held appears, as a "Funds held in trust" liability itemized by program.
2. **Montgomery County Green Bank (R8, FY2025)** — three models in one audit: government revolving capital as an explicit *"funds held on behalf of the county"* liability (because unspent funds revert to the county), a $65.9M federal GGRF subgrant as deferred revenue, and partner-lender loan portfolios entirely off-book with only a capped loss-reserve exposure.
3. **Florida Development Finance Corporation (R6, FY2021)** — issuer-of-record netting: *"FDFC is merely a conduit issuer of bonds issued on behalf of borrowers… Assigned payments are not included in the accompanying basic financial statements."* GASB framework, so analogy — but it is the cleanest demonstration that legal obligor/lender-of-record status does not force recognition when repayment comes solely from borrowers.
4. **Community Vision Capital & Consulting (R2, FY2025)** — "Funds held in trust" as a gross agency asset with an exactly matching liability for pools managed "on behalf of private foundations, local government agencies, and other third parties," plus $218M of NMTC allocation deployed through unconsolidated sub-CDEs with fee-only income.
5. **NT CDFI, Inc. (R5, 990-anchored)** — the purest structural illustration: the manager of the National Trust's revolving loan fund reports loans of $526,352 offset dollar-for-dollar by "Due to National Trust" $526,353.

## 2. Strongest support for third-party institutional / philanthropic loan capital

- **R1 (NJCC):** Bank of America-capitalized pre-development fund and the HCDN trade-association SEED fund are administered off-balance-sheet — named institutional funders, explicit "services and administers the program on behalf of" language.
- **R2 (Community Vision):** intermediary pools for "private foundations… and other third parties" under the funder's conditions.
- **R5 (NT CDFI):** parent fund built from "donor contributions, program related investments from foundations, and low-interest loans from financial institutions," administered by a zero-equity manager.
- **R9 (New Hope) / R13j (Merritt):** the ASC 958-810 route for pooled third-party capital — manager in form, no consolidation where investors hold substantive kick-out/participating rights; fee income only. (Equity capital, so analogy.)
- **R13a (Church Loan Fund):** retirement-plan capital funding church mortgages through a near-zero-equity originator/administrator — the only self-described lender-of-record administrator found (990-only; pursue its audit — see unverified_leads #8).

## 3. Strongest support for government grant-funded or credit-enhancement layers

- **R8 (MCGB):** county revolving fund held as agency liability with an express reversion clause — the cleanest government-capital agency treatment in the set; plus the loss-reserve note showing a *capped first-loss* posture against partner-lender loans: *"funds that are held by the Organization for the purpose of reimbursing the lender for future loans which may default."*
- **R3 (Appalachian Community Capital / Green Bank for Rural America):** $500M EPA GGRF award "included in the restricted cash… with a corresponding amount as deferred revenue" — federal lending capital as liability-until-earned, not equity.
- **R12 (VHFA):** even a fully grossing HFA segregates "Funds held for federal programs" as a liability caption.
- **Cautionary contrast — R10 (NHCLF):** USDA regranting run through revenue/expense ("Grants - pass-through $41.8M") because the administrator selects recipients. The agent-vs-recipient line under ASC 958-605 is discretion/variance power; ministerial administration supports agency, discretionary regranting supports contribution accounting. Draft the target org's program agreements accordingly.

## 4. Support for gross agency asset / matching agency liability presentation

- **R2 (Community Vision):** "Funds held in trust" $7,454,920 on both sides of the statement of financial position, with a rollforward netting out "grant and administrative fees" kept by the administrator.
- **R5 (NT CDFI 990):** exact asset/liability match ("Due to National Trust").
- **NTHP Note 6 (within R5):** *"Amounts held for others, reported as both an asset and liability in the accompanying consolidated statements of financial position"* — audited FASB language for the both-sides presentation (endowments).
- **R13a (Church Loan Fund) / R13f (Sandhurst):** near-zero-equity administrators whose loans are offset by liabilities to the capital owner (990-anchored).
- **R11 (Renaissance):** "Due to FHLB - Small Business Boost" as a distinct pass-through liability caption separate from the entity's own notes payable.

## 5. Support for full exclusion from loans receivable and debt

- **R1 (NJCC):** the explicit non-recognition policy note — the only example found that says in words that the loans are not recorded.
- **R6 (FDFC):** conduit debt and assigned repayment streams both excluded; note-only rollforward of outstanding balances (GASB analogy, issuer of record).
- **R8 (MCGB):** partner-lender loans never recognized; only the capped reserve liability.
- **R9 (New Hope):** deconsolidation of manager-controlled-in-form funds under ASC 958-810-25.
- **R13b (CT CDFI Alliance):** servicing-only entity with a zero balance sheet.
- **R13d (SBA 504 CDCs):** an entire regulatory class in which the CDC is lender of record on debenture-funded loans that do not appear on its statements; visible in 990 fingerprints (servicing-fee-dominant revenue, tiny balance sheets).

## 6. Cautionary examples an auditor could use against the position

- **R7 (Pathway Lending, D):** a state-program administrator that recognizes every program loan as its own asset, with a supplemental schedule attributing $214M of loans to named state funds. Distinguish on: the state money was a conditional grant *to Pathway*; repayments revolve to Pathway; Pathway holds the allowance (full credit risk).
- **R12 (VHFA, D):** the HFA gross-up model — own bonds, own loans, own credit risk. Distinguish on ownership of collections and general-recourse debt.
- **R10 (NHCLF):** pass-through-as-revenue where the administrator has discretion (agent-vs-recipient risk).
- **R4 (Springboard CalHome):** state-grant-funded revolving loans recognized as the entity's own assets where repayments revolve into a reuse account the entity controls — an auditor will probe who controls recycled collections in the target structure.
- **ASC 860 angle:** no example in the set used secured-borrowing presentation for administered pools; the risk arises only if the target org first *owns* the loans and transfers them (then 860 applies). Where the org never controls the receivable or the collections (agency from inception), 860 never attaches — keep the fact pattern on the 958-605 agency side.

## 8. Honest assessment

**Are clean net precedents inherently thinly disclosed?** Yes. The screen confirms the premise: netting is quiet. Of 12 audited-statement records, only NJCC states the non-recognition policy in words; the others reveal it through captions ("funds held in trust/for others/on behalf of"), matching asset-liability pairs, zero-equity structures, or the absence of a portfolio the narrative describes. Several strong 990 fingerprints (Church Loan Fund, CT CDFI Alliance, the 504 CDC class) have no public audit at all. Practitioners doing this quietly outnumber those documenting it.

**How far can published precedent carry the position?** Precedent here corroborates but cannot carry. The load-bearing analysis remains first-principles: under FASB Concepts Statement No. 8 the administered loans are not the NFP's assets (no control of the economic benefits — collections belong to the funder through the waterfall) and the program capital is not its liability in a general-recourse sense (the obligation is to remit what is collected, which is exactly what the "funds held for others" captions express); under ASC 958-605's agent/intermediary guidance (descended from SFAS 136), an NFP that receives resources as an agent — without variance power/discretion — recognizes an asset only for what it holds and a matching liability, never contribution revenue or the underlying program assets. The precedents then do three jobs: (i) NJCC shows a Big Four firm signing the exact policy sentence; (ii) MCGB/ACC/VHFA show the government-capital variants (agency liability, deferred revenue, "funds held for federal programs"); (iii) FDFC and the 504 CDC class blunt the "but you're the lender of record" objection. The counterexamples (Pathway, VHFA core, NHCLF pass-through, Springboard CalHome) map the boundary: gross-up follows whenever the entity owns the collections, keeps the recycled capital, or bears the credit risk — so the target org's documents must keep those three features with the funder.

**Methodology and universe (evidence of a systematic search).** Universe: 593 EINs from 15 ProPublica Nonprofit Explorer search queries plus NTEE tagging (L20/L21/L25/L80/S30/S31/S43); 310 organizations had e-filed 990/990-EZ data (817 filings parsed from IRS e-file XML). Screens: F1 custodial-amount arithmetic (4 hits), F2 portfolio-vs-assets ratio (6), F3 servicing-fee-dominance/mismatch (9), F4 government/institutional program administration text (11), plus Schedule D agency-caption scan (34 raw, mostly benign affiliate due-tos). 28 entities flagged; documents retrieved for 15 (22 PDFs: entity websites, Federal Audit Clearinghouse single audits, a state auditor filing, a Candid e-file, an investment-note prospectus); 14 were 990-only; 7 flagged entities were rejected on reading (rejected.csv) under the kill rule. That a systematic, multi-signal sweep of the national CDFI/loan-fund universe surfaces roughly a dozen documentable net-presentation examples — but finds them consistently across independent structures (CDFI pools, conduit issuers, green banks, CDEs, 504 CDCs, denominational funds) and across three decades of terminology — is itself evidence that net/agency presentation is an accepted, if sparsely worded, practice for administered third-party loan capital, and that the absence of loud precedent reflects disclosure economics, not rejection of the accounting.
