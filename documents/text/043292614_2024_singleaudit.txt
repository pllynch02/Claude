Opinion  
We have audited the consolidated financial statements of New Hope Community Capital, Inc. and 
Subsidiaries, which comprise the consolidated statements of financial position as of December 31, 
2024 and 2023, and the related consolidated statements of activities, functional expenses,  and cash 
flows for the years then ended, and the related notes to the consolidated financial statements. 
In our opinion, the accompanying consolidated financial statements present fairly, in all material 
respects, the consolidated financial position of New Hope Community Capital, Inc. and Subsidiaries as 
of December 31, 2024 and 2023, and the changes in its net assets and its cash flows for the years then 
ended in accordance with accounting principles generally accepted in the United States of America. 
Basis for Opinion 
We conducted our audits in accordance with auditing standards generally accepted in the United States 
of America ("GAAS") and our audit for the year ended December 31, 2024 in accordance with the 
standards applicable to financial audits contained in Government Auditing Standards , issued by the 
Comptroller General of United States. Our responsibilities under those standards are further described 
in the Auditor's Responsibilities for the Audit of the Financial Statements section of our report. We are 
required to be independent of New Hope Community Capital, Inc. and Subsidiaries and to meet our 
other ethical responsibilities, in accordance with t he relevant ethical requirements relating to our audits. 
We believe that the audit evidence we have obtained is sufficient and appropriate to provide a basis for 
our audit opinion. The financial statements of the subsidiaries were not audited in accordance with 
Government Auditing Standards . The 2023 financial statements were not required to be audited in 
accordance with Government Auditing Standards.  
Responsibilities of Management for the Financial Statements 
Management is responsible for the preparation and fair presentation of the consolidated financial 
statements in accordance with accounting principles generally accepted in the United States of 
America, and for the design, implementation, and maintenance of internal control relevant to the 
preparation and fair presentation of consolidated financial statements that are free from material 
misstatement, whether due to fraud or error. 
In preparing the consolidated financial statements, management is required to evaluate whether there 
are conditions or events, considered in the aggregate, that raise substantial doubt about New Hope 
Community Capital, Inc. and Subsidiaries' ability to continue as a going concern for one year after the 
date that the consolidated financial statements are available to be issued.

Note 1 - Organization and nature of operations 
The consolidated financial statements include the accounts and transactions of New Hope 
Community Capital, Inc. and Subsidiaries (the "Corporation").  
Organization and business 
New Hope Community Capital, Inc. is a Massachusetts, nonprofit corporation organized to promote 
community development by improving the social  and economic condition of underserved persons 
who lack access to capital and financial services, which qualifies as an organization formed for 
charitable purposes under Section 501(c)(3) of the Internal Revenue Code ("IRC") and is not a 
private foundation under Section 509(a)(2). In furtherance of its primary purpose, the Corporation 
provides services to borrowers and investors and capital for affordable housing and community 
development activities in low-income communities or that serve low-income persons in the form of 
equity investments, grants, or loans. 
On December 23, 2014, the Corporation became certified as a Community Development Financial 
Institution ("CDFI") from the U.S. Treasury's Community Development Financial Institutions Fund 
("CDFI Fund") for low-income targeted populations in various states. The CDFI certification status 
will be effective, unless, as a result of a review of the Corporation by the CDFI Fund, the CDFI 
Fund, in its sole discretion, provides notification of termination of certification. As of the date of the 
independent auditor's report, no such notification has been received. 
Note 2 - Significant accounting policies 
Basis of presentation 
The accompanying consolidated financial statement s are presented in accordance with accounting 
principles generally accepted in the United States of America ("GAAP"), as codified by the Financial 
Accounting Standards Board ("FASB ASC"). 
Principles of consolidation 
The consolidated financial statements include the a ccounts of the Corporation and its wholly-owned 
subsidiaries. All significant inter-company transactions and accounts have been eliminated in 
consolidation. 
Investment in affiliated entities  
New Hope Fund VII, LLC (the "Fund") is a Massachus etts Limited Liability Company organized to 
acquire, hold, dispose of, and otherwise deal with Local Partnership Interests ("Local 
Partnerships"), which own properties that generate low-income housing tax credits ("LIHTC"). 
Generally, ASC 958-810-25 requires consolidation of  limited liability companies, or similar entities 
by the managing member of that entity under the presumption that the managing member controls 
the limited member entity. The presumption of c ontrol by a managing member can be overcome if 
the limited members are able to exercise substantive kick-out or participating rights. The 
Corporation does not consolidate limited liability co mpanies or similar entities in which it owns a 
managing member interest and the presumption of control by the managing member interest is able 
to be overcome. The Corporation reassesses whether or not it holds a controlling financial interest 
in limited liability companies or similar entities on an annual basis. The Fund has been accounted 
for under the equity method of accounting.

The Fund's principal investment objective is to provide a new investment vehicle that will advance 
the charitable purposes of increasing the pool of equity available for investments in affordable 
housing and of making such equity available to disadvantaged populations or underserved 
communities. The Local Partnerships were awarded LIHTC pursuant to IRC Section 42 ("Section 
42"), which regulates the use of the properties as to occupant eligibility and unit gross rent, among 
other requirements. 
The Corporation owns an interest in various entities ranging from .01% to 99.99%. The interests are 
accounted for under the equity method. Under the equity method, the investment is reflected at 
cost, adjusted for the Corporation's share of the investee's income or loss. The Corporation's share 
of the loss exceeded its original investment amount in several investments. The Corporation did not 
record these losses since the Corporation has not guaranteed obligations of the investee, nor is 
otherwise committed to provide further financial support. If any distributions are received from these 
affiliates, investment in affiliated entities is adjusted accordingly.  
Impairment in investment in Local Partnerships 
The Corporation reviews its investment in Local  Partnerships for impairment whenever events or 
changes in circumstances indicate that the carrying value of such investment may not be 
recoverable. Recoverability is measured by a com parison of the carrying amount of the investment 
to the sum of the total amount of the remaining tax credits allocated to the Corporation, the 
realizable income tax benefit of net operating losses and any estimated residual value of the 
investment. 
Use of estimates 
The preparation of consolidated financial statements in  conformity with GAAP accounting principles 
generally accepted in the United States of America requires management to make estimates and 
judgments that affect the reported amounts of assets and liabilities and disclosures of contingencies 
at the date of the consolidated financial statements and revenue and expenses recognized during 
the reporting period. Significant estimates are inherent in the preparation of these consolidated 
financial statements in a number of areas, including determining estimate of potential losses 
relating to loans. Actual results could differ from these estimates. 
Revenue recognition 
The Corporation earns revenue as follows: 
Asset management fees 
The Corporation is entitled to asset management fees for providing services in accordance with 
the operating agreements of the Local Partnerships. The fees are recognized as revenue when 
earned. 
State tax credit sales 
The Corporation sells state tax credits to other companies. All revenue is recognized at the time 
of sale. During the years ended December 31, 2024 and 2023, state tax credit sales of 
$15,221,000 and $3,148,444, respectively, were recognized on the accompanying consolidated 
statements of activities. 
Interest income 
Interest income is accrued as earned in accordance with the contractual terms of the loan 
agreements.

Origination fees 
The Corporation earns origination fees charged to its borrowers pursuant to the terms of select 
loans that it makes. Loan origination fees are capitalized and amortized to interest income as a 
component of loans receivable over the term of the related loan using the straight-line method. 
Contribution and government grants and contracts 
Contributions, including unconditional promises to give (pledges), are recorded as revenue on 
the date received or pledged. Contributions are recorded as net assets with donor restrictions if 
they are received with donor stipulations that limit their use as to purpose or time. Conditional 
promises to give are not recognized until they become unconditional; that is, when the 
conditions on which they depend are substantially met. A transfer of assets that is conditional 
contribution is treated as refundable advance until the conditions have been substantially met or 
explicitly waived by the donor.  
Contributed goods are recognized as revenue at their estimated fair value at date of receipt and 
expensed when used. Contributed services are recognized as revenue if the services create or 
enhance nonfinancial assets or require a spec ialized skill, are provided by individuals 
possessing those skills, and typically need to be purchased if not provided by donation. 
Contributed services that do not meet the above criteria are not recognized as revenues and are 
not reported in the accompanying consolidated financial statements. There were no donated 
goods or contributed services for the years ended December 31, 2024 and 2023.  
The Corporation also receives government grants. These government agreements are 
evaluated as to whether they qualify as contributions or exchange transactions as defined by 
GAAP. Government grants and contracts that are considered unconditional contributions are 
recognized immediately. Government grants and contracts that are considered conditional 
contributions are not recognized as revenue until the barriers to entitlement are overcome. 
Government grants and contracts that are considered exchange transactions are recognized as 
revenue when services have been provided in accordance with the terms of the agreements. 
Contribution and grant income in the amount of $19,544,729 and $976,265 was recognized for 
the years ended December 31, 2024 and 2023. 
Classification of net assets  
Net assets without donor restrictions:  Net assets that are not subject to donor-imposed 
restrictions and may be expended for any purpose in performing the primary objectives of the 
organization. These net assets may be used at the discretion of the Corporation. 
Net assets with donor restrictions:  Net assets subject to stipulations imposed by donors and 
grantors. Some donor restrictions are temporary in nature; those restrictions will be met by the 
actions of the Corporation or by the passage of time. Other donor restrictions are perpetual in 
nature, whereby the donor has stipulated the Corporation be maintained in perpetuity. 
Donor restricted contributions are reported as increases in net assets with donor restrictions. 
When a restriction expires, net assets are reclassified from net assets with donor restrictions to 
net assets without donor restrictions on the accompanying consolidated statements of activities. 
The Corporation elects to recognize donor restricted net asset as a change in net assets without 
donor restrictions if the restrictions is satisfied in the same period.

Concentration of credit risk  
The Corporation maintains cash in one financial institution with insurance provided by the Federal 
Deposit Insurance Corporation ("FDIC") up to $250,000 at each institution. The Corporation 
monitors the creditworthiness of this institution and has not experienced any credit losses in its cash 
and cash equivalents. 
Loans and interest receivable 
The Corporation makes loans to community based not-for-profit and for-profit mission aligned 
affordable housing developers, community organizations and certain affiliates for the purpose of 
supporting low-income communities. Loans are primarily for the purpose of acquiring, renovating 
and/or constructing multi-family residential housing or community development projects. The loans 
are primarily made up of the types described in No te 4. The loans are generally collateralized by 
real estate. The majority of the loans are to be repaid from surplus cash. A balloon payment is 
required for any unpaid principal balance when the property is sold, or at the stated maturity date.  
Loans are carried at their unpaid principal balance, less an allowance for credit losses to reflect 
potentially uncollectable balances including potential losses relating to impaired loans. The 
allowance for credit losses is based upon management's periodic evaluation of the underwriting 
criteria used to initially underwrite the loan as well as other credit factors, economic conditions, 
historic loss experience other risks inherent in t he overall portfolio such as geographic or sponsor 
concentration risks as well as trends. The allowance is increased through a provision for loan 
losses which is charged to expense and reduced by charge-offs, net of recoveries. Loans are 
charged off when repayment is not expected to occur. Additionally, the Corporation considers 
whether borrowers are experiencing financial difficulty and whether delays in payment are 
insignificant.  
Interest received on nonaccrual loans, generally is either applied against principal or reported as 
interest income, according to management's judgement as to the collectability of principal. 
Write-off of accrued interest receivable are recognized by reversing interest income. During the 
year ended December 31, 2024, $126,774 of accrued interest receivable was written off. No write-
off was incurred during the year ended December 31, 2023. 
The Corporation also elected not to measure an allowance for credit losses for accrued interest 
receivables. For all classes of loans receivable, the accrual of interest is discontinued when the 
contractual payment of principal or interest has become 90 days past due, unless the loan is well 
secured and in the process of collection or management has doubts about further collectability of 
principal or interest even though the instrument is performing.

The Corporation makes programmatic loans to community based not-for-profit and for-profit mission 
aligned affordable housing developers, community organizations and certain affiliates for the 
purpose of supporting low-income communities. Programmatic loans are for the purpose of 
acquiring, renovating and/or constructing multi-family residential housing or community 
development projects. The programmatic loans are primarily made up of the types described in

At the time of origination of the progra mmatic loans, management assesses the programmatic 
loans to determine if they qualify as a) a loan receivable, b) an inherent contribution, or c) in part a 
contribution and in part a loan receivable. The assessment is made based on management's best 
judgment after evaluation of facts and circumstances available at the time of first accounting for the 
loan. As further described in Note 4, management has determined that various programmatic loans 
qualify as entirely an inherent contribution, as the Corporation does not intend to pursue collection if 
the loan becomes past due, and the borrower's creditworthiness was not considered by 
management. For loans determined entirely  to be contributions, contribution expense is recognized 
at origination for the full balance of the loan. Loan repayments will be recognized as revenue in the 
period received by the Corporation. 
Income taxes 
The Corporation is recognized as a 501(c)(3) charitable organization and is exempt from income 
taxes with respect to charitable activities, except for unrelated business income. There was no 
unrelated business income during the years ended December 31, 2024 and 2023. Accordingly, no 
provision or benefit for income taxes has been recorded in the accompanying consolidated financial 
statements. Tax returns required to be completed by tax-exempt entities are filed with the Internal 
Revenue Service ("IRS") and other taxing authorities. These income tax returns are subject to 
examination by the IRS for a period of three years.  While no income tax returns are currently being 
examined by the IRS, tax years since 2021 remain open for examination.  
The two wholly-owned subsidiaries are Subchapter C corporations ("C Corps") that are subject to 
taxation at the federal and state levels. Deferred taxes are computed based on the difference 
between the consolidated financial statements and income tax basis of assets and liabilities using 
enacted marginal tax rates. As of and for the years ended December 31, 2024 and 2023, the tax 
expense, deferred taxes and valuation allowances were immaterial. 
Accordingly, these consolidated financial statements do not reflect a provision for income taxes and 
there are no other tax positions which must be considered for disclosure. 
Prepaid expenses 
The Corporation has certain prepaid administrativ e expenses. The Corporation initially records 
these expenditures as a prepaid expense and charges it to expense as services are performed. 
Debt issuance costs 
Debt issuance costs, net of accumulated amortization, are reported as a direct deduction from the 
face amount of the notes payable to which such costs relate. Amortization of debt issuance costs is 
reported as a component of interest expense and is computed using an imputed interest rate on the 
related loan. 
Cash and cash equivalents 
The Corporation considers all highly-liquid instruments purchased with a maturity of three months or 
less to be cash equivalents.  
Designated cash  
The Corporation considers designated cash to in clude cash amounts internally designated by 
management for uses to fulfill its certain program purposes. As of December 31, 2024 and 2023, 
the total amount of designated cash was $10,078,938 and $0, respectively.

Predevelopment 
Predevelopment loans are primarily unsecured loans to borrowers to allow borrowers to advance 
affordable housing and community development projects. These loans generally have terms of 
three years with two one-year extensions allowed, and they are expected to be repaid at the 
project's initial closing.

receivable on the accompanying consolidated statements of financial position. 
Construction  
Construction loans are primarily secured, interest-only loans to borrowers to allow borrowers to 
advance affordable housing and community development projects in Chicago. These loans 
generally have construction terms of two to eight years and they are expected to be repaid at the 
project's stabilization. Some of these loans are Construction to Permanent loans, which convert to 
permanent loans with distinct terms at Stabilization. 
As of December 31, 2024 and 2023, construction loans of $14,516,741 and $7,950,096,

consolidated statements of financial position. As of December 31, 2024 and 2023, accrued interest 
of $474,825 and $261,104, respectively, remains outstanding and is included in interest receivable 
on the accompanying consolidated statements of financial position. 
State tax credit 
State tax credit loans are loans where a project sponsor has transferred state tax credits to the 
Corporation to sell to an investor with the Corporation then lending the sale proceeds to the project.

refinancing proceeds. State tax credit loans are secured with a mortgage on the real estate 
involved.

Allowance for credit losses 
The allowance for credit losses for total loans receivable is based on management's review of each 
individual loan. Management reviews cash flow projec tions of the underlying projects with potential 
future impact on the recent pandemic on the commercial real estate market, considers historical 
loan performance and explicit guarantee as well as implicit guarantee on the funding sources, and 
determines a reasonable amount of the loan balance that will possibly be collected and records a 
specific provision for credit losses. During the years ended December 31, 2024 and 2023, $897,097 
and $176,265, respectively, was recorded as provision for credit losses for commercial loans and 
homeownership loans. 
Additionally, management has determined, due to risks inherent in the predevelopment loan 
portfolio funded by specific sources partially offset by the positive experience on the loans' prior 
performance and consideration of general uncertainti es in the broader economic environment, that 
a loan loss reserve of 3.5% should be established and applied to the unpaid principal balance of the 
specific predevelopment loans outstanding. In m anagement's judgment, due to historical loan loss 
experience, the allowance for credit losses is adequate to absorb credit losses inherent in the loan 
portfolio. During the year ended December 31, 2024, $120,909 was recorded as recovery of 
provision for credit losses for predevelopment loans. During the year ended December 31, 2023,  
$7,418, was recorded as provision for credit losses for predevelopment loans.
